The whole loop, at a glance

Getting a song onto tape

1

PC straight to tape

PC headphone out, a resistor divider, a DIN plug into the recorder. You press REC + START by hand. No ESP at all.

Needs: one DIN plug, a 3.5 mm cable, 2 resistors.

smallfirst test
2

Tape ESP as a USB sound card

The S3 has real USB: the PC sees a "Tape" sound device, FluidSynth plays to it, the ESP passes it to the PCM5102 and on to the recorder. No PC headphone cable, and the ESP knows exactly when the song starts.

Needs: PCM5102, DIN plug, USB audio code on the S3.

big
3

Tape ESP plays the sound itself

The ESP makes the sounds (its own small synth, "direction B" from the start of this project), driven by the toy's MIDI. Works without the PC. Sound is simpler than FluidSynth.

Needs: PCM5102, a synth on the ESP.

big
4

Start recording on a bar line

You press REC + START with the motor's power held off; the tape ESP switches the motor on (MOSFET) exactly at the next bar, so the tape starts with the song, not with silence.

Needs: open the recorder, a MOSFET in the motor wire.

medium
5

Count-in on the tape

Before the song, the ESP records 4 short clicks at the song's tempo. Played back later, the toy hears them and knows tempo and bar 1 before the music starts, then follows the kicks.

Needs: idea 2 or 3 (the ESP makes the audio).

smallonce 2/3 work

Playing it back

6

The toy follows the tape

The deck's playback signal (DIN, or inside before the volume) goes into the ESP's ADC. It finds the kicks and moves the beat, metronome, looper and fireworks along with the tape, drift included. Your live keys don't get in: they never pass through that wire.

Needs: a cap + 2 resistors to the ADC, beat-finding code.

medium
7

Tape as a backing track

Record a beat + bass to tape, play keys and loops live over it. With idea 6 the new layers line up with the tape; without it, only for short parts (tape drifts 1-3%).

Needs: idea 1, better with 6.

small
8

Real tape stop

The Tape chick (6) fakes a slowdown with a pitch bend. With the motor on a MOSFET, the ESP really slows the motor down: the actual tape groans to a halt.

Needs: MOSFET on the playback deck's motor.

medium
9

Wow on a knob

Same MOSFET, the motor's speed wobbled slightly with PWM: real lo-fi warble on whatever plays from tape, its depth on an Effects pot.

Needs: idea 8's wiring; test if the motor likes PWM.

medium

Its speaker, without the PC

A PCM5102 on the toy's ESP into a small amp module (PAM8403) and the boom box's speaker. With idea 3's synth the whole thing plays alone, no PC.

Needs: an amp module (the old electronics go, 2026-11-26).

Before any of it

  1. The SL 53's DIN socket has 7 pins (you counted). A 5-pin plug with its pins in a half circle (180°) normally fits a 7-pin socket; pins 6 and 7 stay unused usual for DIN, check with the plug. On the standard tape socket, 1 = record in, 2 = ground, 3 = playback out, 4 / 5 = the right channel's in / out standard, not checked on this recorder. What 6 and 7 do on the SL 53: unknown, measure before connecting anything there.
  2. The DIN record input expects a much smaller signal than line level usual for DIN, to measure: a PCM5102 or PC output needs a divider in front of it, or the recording distorts.
  3. A cassette that plays: hear the SL 53 and the boom box (after its belt) play a known song.
  4. The PCM5102 boards: check the back's jumpers H1L-H4L are bridged (XSMT unbridged = muted, the usual "no sound" on this board) and SCK goes to GND listing photos + common for this board, check on yours.
  5. The PCM5102 goes through esp32-hw-checks first, like every new module, and gets a Homebox entry (your OK).
